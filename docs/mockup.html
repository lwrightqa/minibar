Dark mode is the Low Glare treatment: picking it starts from Low Glare's colors, tints and sentence-case headlines, with the fonts you've chosen. How you'd control it

        

          
Touch
Tap cycles status, a swipe steps back or forward, and holding opens a quick menu with light, calendar, Wi-Fi and power. On the Pomodoro screen, a tap starts or pauses the timer and holding opens timer options. On a call or meeting screen, a tap goes back to your own status; the call or meeting is set aside until it ends. A dark screen wakes on the first tap, and every action shows a short confirmation.

          
Pomodoro
25-minute focus sessions, 5-minute breaks and a 15-minute break after every fourth session, all adjustable. Focus shows in orange so people know not to interrupt; breaks turn the bar teal or blue to say it's a good moment. Each finished session earns a red tomato, upcoming sessions show as faded tomatoes, and the one in progress ripens like a real tomato as you focus: color breaks at the bottom and spreads up to the stem, through yellow and orange to red. The timer keeps running when you switch to another status, with a small countdown in the corner, and the bar jumps back to it when a phase ends. A call or calendar meeting pauses it, and the bar makes no sound while one is on. A ringing alarm goes quiet and chimes once when it's over. Ticking during focus is optional and off by default: one gentle tick a second, Soft or Medium, set on the Remote or under Settings in the timer menu. It's silent while the timer is paused, on breaks, while the alarm rings, during calls and meetings, while the screen is dark, and when the bar is powered off.

          
Side buttons
BOOT jumps to the next status without looking; on a call or meeting screen it goes back to your own status, like a tap. A press of PWR turns the screen off and on, holding it for 3 seconds powers the bar off, and a press turns it back on. If the screen is dark or an alarm is ringing, the first press of either button only wakes the screen or silences the alarm.

          
Power
On battery, the software is what keeps the bar switched on, so it also handles switching off: hold PWR for 3 seconds, or use Power in the quick menu, which also has Restart. A watchdog restarts the bar if the software ever freezes, so it can't get stuck on, and it shuts itself down before the battery runs flat. On USB the bar always has power, so powering off puts it into a deep sleep until you press PWR. While it's off, the Remote page can't reach it and says so.

          
Wi-Fi setup
On first start the bar shows a QR code. Scanning it joins your phone to the bar's own network, where a page lets you choose the office Wi-Fi, enter the password and paste your calendar's secret iCal address (optional). You can also skip Wi-Fi; statuses and the Pomodoro work without it, but the calendar and the Remote don't.

          
Phone remote
The device serves a small page at tinybar.local on your Wi-Fi, like the Remote panel on this page. Its Automatic status section has a switch for each source, holds your calendar's secret address, and shows what the bar is showing and why.

          
Calendar
Paste your calendar's secret iCal address on the Remote, or during Wi-Fi setup (Google Calendar › Settings › your calendar › Integrate calendar › "Secret address in iCal format"). Meetings then show as In a meeting on their own and fill in Next up. Once saved, the address is never shown again; the Remote shows a short masked form with Replace and Remove. Show meeting titles is off by default, so the bar shows times only, and events marked Private never show a title. The bar syncs about every 10 minutes, or when you choose Sync now, and switches at each meeting's exact start and end time. Skipping Wi-Fi turns the calendar off; if Wi-Fi drops, the bar follows its last synced copy.

          
Automatic status
Two sources, each with its own switch on the Remote: meetings from your calendar, and calls from your Mac. On a call wins over In a meeting, which wins over your own status. A tap, swipe, BOOT, flip or a choice on the Remote sets the call or meeting aside until it ends, and a small headset or calendar glyph stays in the corner. Show again, in the quick menu, the timer menu or on the Remote, brings it back, and the next call or meeting always takes over again. A call or meeting that takes over pauses the Pomodoro, and the bar makes no sound while one is on.

          
Mac app (later)
A small menu-bar app, built later, will set On a call whenever your Mac's mic or camera is in use, over USB when the bar is powered from the Mac and otherwise over Wi-Fi. Only whether you're on a call, and optionally the app's name, leaves the Mac. How the Mac app talks to TinyBar

          
Local API
The bar answers on your Wi-Fi at tinybar.local: POST /api/call sets or ends On a call, and GET /api/status says what the bar is showing and which sources are on. The same JSON works one line at a time over USB serial. It never returns the calendar address.

          
Flip
Turning the bar over always does three things: the layout turns the right way up, any alarm goes quiet, and the Pomodoro starts whatever it's waiting for (the next session or break, or a paused timer). A timer that's already running is left alone, so a flip never skips anything. On a call or meeting screen it also shows the Pomodoro, which sets the call or meeting aside.

          
Alarm
When a session or break ends, the bar flashes and chimes every few seconds until you tap, press a button or flip it, and gives up after a minute. With auto-start on, it chimes once and carries on. During a call or meeting it flashes without the chime. The mock-up plays the chime in your browser.

        

      

      

        
Hardware it uses

        

          
Display
3.49" IPS, 172 × 640, AXS15231B over QSPI

          
Touch
AXS15231B over I²C

          
MCU
ESP32-S3R8, 8 MB PSRAM, 16 MB flash

          
Clock
PCF85063 RTC, synced over NTP

          
Motion
QMI8658 6-axis IMU

          
Audio
ES8311 codec + speaker

          
Power
USB-C, or 3.7 V Li-ion on MX1.25 (pin 2 +), charges at 1.2 A

How the Mac app talks to TinyBar

    
The bar's side is ready now, so the Mac app can be added without changing the bar. The app sends one thing: whether you're on a call, and optionally the app's name. Over Wi-Fi it uses the bar's local web address; when the bar is powered from the Mac it uses the USB cable instead, which also works without Wi-Fi.

    
POST http://tinybar.local/api/call
Content-Type: application/json
Authorization: Bearer <token>        (reserved for pairing, not checked yet)

{"active": true, "app": "Slack"}     →  200 {"ok": true, "showing": "call"}
{"active": false}                    →  200 {"ok": true, "showing": "own"}
                                        ("meeting" if a calendar meeting is on)
When Calls from your Mac is off      →  403 {"ok": false, "error": "calls_off"}
Not valid JSON, or no "active"       →  400 {"ok": false, "error": "bad_request"}

GET http://tinybar.local/api/status
→ {"showing": "call", "own": "busy",
   "call": {"active": true, "app": "Slack", "via": "usb", "since": "2026-10-04T14:12:00-07:00"},
   "meeting": {"active": false, "next": "15:00"},
   "sources": {"calendar": true, "mac": true}}

USB serial: one JSON object per line, UTF-8, ending in \n. The bar answers each line with one line.
→ {"cmd": "hello"}                              ← {"ok": true, "device": "TinyBar", "fw": "1.0.0"}
→ {"cmd": "call", "active": true, "app": "Slack"}  ← {"ok": true, "showing": "call"}
→ {"cmd": "status"}                             ← the same object as GET /api/status

    
    
      
  • active is required. app is optional: the bar shows up to 24 characters of it, and the Mac app can leave it out.
    • 
      
  • The app sends its state on every change and repeats it every 30 seconds. After 90 seconds without a message, the bar ends On a call on its own, so it can't get stuck.
    • 
      
  • showing is call, meeting, own (your own status, including a call or meeting you set aside), setup or off.
    • 
      
  • No reply ever contains the calendar's secret address. Meeting titles are left out when Show meeting titles is off.
    • 
      
  • If both links work, the app prefers USB. The bar accepts either, and the latest message wins.
