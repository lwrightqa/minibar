Pairing

Phone
Speed

Mac app asks to pair stands in for Pair Over Wi-Fi in the Mac app: the bar shows a code, and you type it here, as you would on the Mac. Plug in over USB sets Link to USB, and a Mac that isn't paired yet pairs at once, with no code. A code lasts 2 minutes and allows 3 tries, and holds one of the bar's 10 places until it ends; Speed (the same as the Remote's Demo speed) runs it 60 times faster. After two failed pairings in a row, the bar waits 30 seconds before it shows another code, then 1, 2, 4 minutes. Phone switches the Remote between paired and its pairing prompt, without codes or toasts.

Help and Wi-Fi

Scan the More help code stands in for pointing the phone at the tour's last card: the phone panel opens the help page the code leads to. Forget "tour seen" makes the bar forget it has shown its tour, so it shows it once more after its next start-up (Restart, or power off and on), as a bar that gets the tour in a firmware update does; First-time Wi-Fi setup forgets it too, and then the tour shows when setup ends. The bar remembers it in this browser. Wi-Fi drops cuts the bar off the office Wi-Fi until Wi-Fi is back: it keeps following its saved calendar, and the Remote can't reach it meanwhile.

Sounds

Hear the bar's sounds side by side, each once at its own level, whatever the settings say. Your computer's speaker isn't the bar's, so compare them with each other, not with the real thing. The bar aims to click within 30 ms of your finger lifting; your browser adds its own delay here.

Design directions

Complete looks for the screen, side by side. The first two are the bar's themes. Bold Signal, white Barlow Condensed capitals on the full status color with a darker info column, is the default: a new bar starts in it. Low Glare Pixel is the alternate theme, the Low Glare layout set entirely in the Handjet pixel font, for when a screen full of color is too bright for the people around you. You switch between them on the bar (hold the screen, then Display, then Theme), on the Remote, or with Apply on their cards here, which follows the Remote's rule (while the bar is off or offline the card says so instead); the card marked On the bar is the bar's theme. The other three are design studies: Apply puts one on the simulator as a preview, mock-up only, and the bar's theme stays as it is. The large preview in each card follows the simulator live; the small ones show Busy and the Pomodoro.

Style

Try fonts, colors and screen modes on the screen. Anything you change here is a preview on the simulator, mock-up only: the bar's theme doesn't change. Your changes stay in this browser. Copy them when you've found a look you like, and send them to me for the firmware.

The simulator opens with the bar's theme. The default, Bold Signal, uses Barlow Condensed for headlines and Barlow for small text on solid color fields; each headline takes the largest of its four sizes that fits (112, 100, 78 or 62 px). The alternate, Low Glare Pixel, sets the Low Glare layout in Handjet: its status words take 102 px (68 px for Back at 12:30 and the alarm), the timer and clock 136 px, and small text 17 px. In both, the digits are all one width, so the timer and clock don't jitter. Switching the theme on the bar or the Remote ends a preview, and Reset to defaults drops your changes and shows the bar's theme as it is.

Handjet and its snapped copy, Handjet Snap17, come first under Pixel fonts, then Bitcount Prop Single, option G's headline font from before the switch to Bold Signal. Pixel fonts snap to whole multiples of their own pixel grid so they stay crisp, and each headline takes the largest of those sizes that fits. Bitcount's digits are drawn in equal-width cells, as the bar's copy of the font would draw them. With a pixel headline font other than Jersey 10 or 15, the line under the headline uses the text font, since the others are too wide or too big for it. Headline case UPPERCASE on Low Glare Pixel shows the UX designer's capitals, with BUSY at 136 px.

Dark mode is the Low Glare treatment: picking it starts from Low Glare's colors, tints and sentence-case headlines, with the fonts you've chosen. The original look is still here: choose Solid color fields, then Big Shoulders Display and Schibsted Grotesk.

Stage names are the USDA's ripeness classes for fresh tomatoes. Tap one to jump to it.

The controls

          
Each control has one main job. The lists say where it does something else. For a 1-minute tour on the bar itself, hold the screen, then tap Help.

        

        
Tap
Touch and lift
Shows your next status.
  • On the Pomodoro: starts or pauses the timer.
  • On a call or meeting: back to your own status. The call or meeting is set aside until it ends.
  • On a menu: runs the tile you tap. A tap off the tiles closes it.

        
Swipe
Slide a finger across the screen
Left for the next status, right for the one before.
  • Left and right follow the screen, even after a flip.
  • On a call or meeting: counts from your own status, and sets the call or meeting aside.
  • On a menu: does nothing, so it can't hit a tile by mistake.

        
Touch and hold
Keep a finger down for half a second
Opens the menu for the screen you're on.
  • On a status, call or meeting: the quick menu.
  • On the Pomodoro: the timer menu.
  • On Wi-Fi setup: Skip, or Start over.

        
Flip
Turn the bar over
Starts the Pomodoro, or whatever it's waiting for, and quiets any alarm. The screen turns the right way up.
  • A running timer is left alone.
  • On a call or meeting: shows the Pomodoro, which sets the call or meeting aside.

        
BOOT button
Press the BOOT button
Shows your next status, without looking.
  • On the Pomodoro too: next status (a tap starts or pauses instead).
  • On a call or meeting: back to your own status, like a tap.
  • On a menu: closes it and shows your next status.

        
PWR button, press
Press and let go
Turns the screen off, or back on.
  • Timers, calls and meetings carry on while it's dark.
  • A dark screen also wakes on a touch or BOOT.

        
PWR button, hold
Keep it down for 3 seconds
Powers MiniBar off. A PWR press starts it again.
  • Keep holding shows on the screen, and touches and BOOT wait. Let go early and nothing changes.
  • A running Pomodoro stops. Today's tomatoes, your settings and paired devices stay.

        
On any screen
  • Ringing alarm: any tap, swipe, hold, button or flip answers it. BOOT and PWR only quiet it.
  • Dark screen: the first tap or button press only wakes it.
  • A 6-digit code on the bar: a phone or Mac asked to pair. Any touch or button cancels it; a flip cancels it, then flips.
  • Wi-Fi setup: a tap or BOOT moves on where the screen says so.
  • The quick tour: tap or BOOT for the next tip, swipe right to go back, hold to leave. Nothing you try in it changes your status.
  • Tap sound: a soft click means the bar took your tap, swipe or tile, and a lower one that a hold opened the menu. It's quiet during calls and meetings and on a dark screen. Turn it off in the Display menu or on the Remote.

        

          
Where the buttons are

          
BOOT and PWR are on the bar's top edge, BOOT on the left and PWR on the right (to be confirmed on the bar). Turned over, they're on the bottom edge. Not sure which is which? The tour's BOOT and PWR tips point at each one.

If something's off

          

            
My status changed by mistake
Swipe right to go back one, or pick it on the Remote. A call or meeting you set aside comes back with Show again in the menu.

            
The screen is upside down
It turns the right way up within a second. If it doesn't: hold the screen, tap Power, then Restart.

            
The screen is dark
Tap it or press PWR. A dark screen keeps everything running.

            
The Remote won't open
Make sure your phone is on the office Wi-Fi, not mobile data. Some Android phones need the IP address: hold the screen, then tap Wi-Fi to see it.

            
"Another device is pairing"
Someone else's code is on the bar. Wait for it to end (2 minutes at most), then try again.

            
The Calendar tile says Off
Add a calendar's secret iCal address on the Remote, or set up Wi-Fi first. If it says Error, the Remote's Calendars list says which one can't be reached.

            
More than one MiniBar in the office
Each bar has its own name, like MiniBar 2A1C. Hold the screen and tap Wi-Fi to see this bar's name and address.

      How you'd control it
      
        
      
          
      Touch
      Tap cycles status, a swipe steps back or forward, and holding opens a quick menu with display (light, theme, Tap sound and Time format), calendar, Wi-Fi, power and Help. Left and right are as the screen faces you, so a swipe works the same way on a bar that's turned over. On the Pomodoro screen, a tap starts or pauses the timer and holding opens timer options. On a call or meeting screen, a tap goes back to your own status; the call or meeting is set aside until it ends. A dark screen wakes on the first tap, and every action shows a short confirmation. On a pairing code, a tap, swipe or hold only cancels it. While Keep holding or Powering off shows, touches are ignored. With Tap sound on (the default), a soft click confirms each tap, swipe and menu tile the bar acts on, and a lower one tells you a hold has opened the menu.
      
          
      Help
      Proposed, from the user's request of 2026-10-05. The bar has a quick tour of its controls, seven tips in a row: Tap, Swipe and BOOT, which you try for real and which only say what they would have done ("Practice only: Busy would become In a meeting"), so nothing changes; Touch and hold, Flip and PWR, which say what they do and end the tour if you try them; and More help, with a QR code for the help page the bar serves at minibar.local/help (at its IP address, so any phone on the office Wi-Fi can open it). A tap, BOOT or a swipe left goes on, a swipe right goes back, and a hold, flip or PWR leaves; it closes on its own after 30 seconds without a touch. It shows by itself once, when a new bar's first setup ends; after that it's under Help in the quick menu, or Settings in the timer menu. It never fills the screen with a status color, makes no sound of its own, and changes nothing: your status, the timer, a call or meeting you set aside and every setting carry on underneath. The Remote says while it's showing, and has a Help link to the same page on every view. Try it with the Simulate panel's Help and Wi-Fi box.
      
          
      Tap sound
      Proposed (the setting's place, beside Theme, decided by the user on 2026-10-05). The screen can't buzz under your finger, so a short, quiet click confirms a touch the bar acted on: a crisp click for a tap, a swipe or a menu tile, when your finger lifts, and a lower one at the moment a hold opens the menu, while your finger is still down. Touches the bar ignores stay silent, so silence means it didn't count. It's never louder than Soft ticking, and it's silent during a call or calendar meeting (even one you set aside), on a dark screen (the touch that wakes it), while starting up or powering off, and for BOOT, PWR, a flip and anything done from the Remote. It's on by default; turn it off in the Display menu (hold, then Display, then Tap sound) or on the Remote's Display section. In the API it's sound.tap_sound. Hear it beside the ticks and the chime with the Simulate panel's Sounds box.
      
          
      Themes
      Proposed, from the user's request of 2026-10-05. The bar has two looks. Bold Signal, the default, fills the screen with the status color behind big white capitals, the strongest signal across the room. Low Glare Pixel, the alternate, is the Low Glare layout set entirely in the Handjet pixel font: a near-black screen with the status in a colored pixel headline, a thin edge bar and a faint side tint, so it gives off far less light toward the people around you and the monitors nearby. Switch on the bar (hold the screen, then Display, then Theme; the Display menu also has Light, Tap sound and Time format) or on the Remote (Display, then Theme). Only the look changes, at once and on whatever screen is up, and a toast confirms it: "Theme · Low Glare Pixel". While the screen isn't free (dark, starting up, in Wi-Fi setup, showing a pairing code, or while PWR is held) the toast waits, as any confirmation from the Remote does; a PWR hold that powers the bar off drops it, and the bar starts in the new theme. Every screen says the same words in both. The bar keeps the theme through Restart and power off and on, and Wi-Fi setup and Forget all leave it alone. In the API it's display.theme.
      
          
      Pomodoro
      25-minute focus sessions, 5-minute breaks and a 15-minute break after every fourth session, all adjustable. Focus shows in orange so people know not to interrupt; breaks turn the bar teal or blue to say it's a good moment. Each finished session earns a red tomato, upcoming sessions show as faded tomatoes, and the one in progress ripens like a real tomato as you focus: color breaks at the bottom and spreads up to the stem, through yellow and orange to red. The timer keeps running when you switch to another status, with a small countdown in the corner, and the bar jumps back to it when a phase ends. A call or calendar meeting pauses it, and the bar makes no sound while one is on. A ringing alarm goes quiet and chimes once when it's over. Ticking during focus is optional and off by default: one gentle tick a second, Soft or Medium, set on the Remote or under Settings in the timer menu. It's silent while the timer is paused, on breaks, while the alarm rings, during calls and meetings, while the screen is dark, and when the bar is powered off.
      
          
      BOOT and PWR
      Both buttons sit on the bar's top long edge, slightly inset, so the bar can rest on that edge turned over without pressing them (turned over, they're on the bottom edge). BOOT jumps to the next status without looking; on a call or meeting screen it goes back to your own status, like a tap. A press of PWR turns the screen off and on, holding it for 3 seconds powers the bar off, and a press turns it back on. If the screen is dark or an alarm is ringing, the first press of either button only wakes the screen or silences the alarm. On a pairing code, BOOT cancels it, and a PWR press cancels it and turns the screen off. While Keep holding or Powering off shows, BOOT does nothing. Neither button makes the Tap sound click; they click on their own.
      
          
      Power
      On battery, the software is what keeps the bar switched on, so it also handles switching off: hold PWR for 3 seconds, or use Power in the quick menu, which also has Restart. A watchdog restarts the bar if the software ever freezes, so it can't get stuck on, and it shuts itself down before the battery runs flat. On USB the bar always has power, so powering off puts it into a deep sleep until you press PWR. While it's off, the Remote page can't reach it and says so.
      
          
      Wi-Fi setup
      On first start the bar shows a QR code. Scanning it joins your phone to the bar's own network, where a page lets you choose the office Wi-Fi, enter the password and paste your calendar's secret iCal address (optional). You can also skip Wi-Fi; statuses and the Pomodoro work without it, but the calendar and the Remote don't. Once it's on the Wi-Fi, the bar shows its name and address (MiniBar 2A1C at minibar.local) on the Connected screen and in the Wi-Fi menu. A second MiniBar on the same network takes the next free address (minibar-2.local) and shows that instead, so you can tell them apart. The default name, MiniBar plus the last four characters of the bar's ID, is proposed. When a new bar's first setup ends, with Connected or with Skip, its quick tour of the controls follows once. If the office Wi-Fi drops later, the bar keeps following its saved calendar and reconnects on its own; the Calendar tile says Offline meanwhile.
      
          
      Phone remote
      The device serves a small page at minibar.local on your Wi-Fi, like the Remote panel on this page, with the bar's name at the top. The first time, it asks you to pair: MiniBar shows a code, and you type it on the phone. Its Display section switches the bar's theme, Bold Signal or Low Glare Pixel, at once, and turns Tap sound on or off. A change made on the Remote while the bar's screen is dark happens underneath and shows, with its confirmation, when the screen is woken. The Help link at the top opens the help page the bar serves, which needs no pairing. Its Automatic status section has a switch for each source, holds your calendar's secret address, and shows what the bar is showing and why. Proposed: its Connect your Mac card has Download for Mac, which hands out the Mac app from the bar's own flash (MiniBar-1.0.zip, with its version and size), and the steps for opening it the first time, when macOS stops an app it can't check until you choose Open Anyway in System Settings › Privacy & Security. The pairing prompt has the same link, since downloading needs no pairing, and on a phone the line under it says the easy way is to open minibar.local on your Mac. A bar that doesn't carry the app, or whose copy is damaged, shows no button, only a line saying why. Paired devices lists every phone, Mac and script that can control the bar, each with Remove. A message with characters the bar can't draw, such as an emoji, says which ones before you send it. Characters nobody can see (an odd-width space, a zero-width space, a direction mark) are tidied away rather than refused, and accents typed as a separate mark are joined to their letter.
      
          
      Calendar
      Paste a calendar's secret iCal address on the Remote, or during Wi-Fi setup (Google Calendar › Settings › the calendar › Integrate calendar › "Secret address in iCal format"). You can add up to 3 calendars; their meetings are merged, soonest first, so there's one Next up and one In a meeting, and a meeting that's in two calendars shows once. With 2 or more calendars, Next up carries a small tag (up to 4 letters, yours to choose) for the calendar it came from. A calendar that can't sync is left out until it works again, and the Remote says which. Once saved, an address is never shown again; the Remote only says "Address saved", with Edit and Remove. Show meeting titles is off by default, so the bar shows times only, and events marked Private never show a title. The bar syncs about every 10 minutes, or when you choose Sync now, and switches at each meeting's exact start and end time. Skipping Wi-Fi turns the calendar off; if Wi-Fi drops, the bar follows its last synced copy.
      
          
      Automatic status
      Two sources, each with its own switch on the Remote: meetings from your calendar, and calls from your Mac. On a call wins over In a meeting, which wins over your own status. A tap, swipe, BOOT, flip or a choice on the Remote sets the call or meeting aside until it ends, and a small headset or calendar glyph stays in the corner. Show again, in the quick menu, the timer menu or on the Remote, brings it back, and the next call or meeting always takes over again. A call or meeting that takes over pauses the Pomodoro, and the bar makes no sound while one is on.
      
          
      Mac app
      A small menu-bar app sets On a call whenever your Mac's mic or camera is in use, over USB when the bar is powered from the Mac and otherwise over Wi-Fi. Only whether you're on a call, and optionally the app's name, leaves the Mac. To use Wi-Fi it pairs once: plugged into the Mac with USB it pairs on its own, or in its menu you choose Connect…, then Pair Over Wi-Fi, and type the code the bar shows. Proposed: the bar carries the app and hands it out, so you get it from the Remote's Download for Mac, in Connect your Mac or on the pairing prompt, with no pairing. It isn't signed through Apple, so the first time you open it macOS stops it until you choose Open Anyway in System Settings › Privacy & Security; the card's steps walk through that, and you only do it once. A bar that doesn't carry the app (never flashed with it, or a damaged copy) says so on the card instead of showing the button. How the Mac app talks to MiniBar
      
          
      Local API
      The bar answers on your Wi-Fi at minibar.local, under /api/v1/: POST /api/v1/call sets or ends On a call, and GET /api/v1/status says what the bar is showing and which sources are on. Proposed: outside the API, GET /mac/app.zip hands out the Mac app, with no token. With pairing, every Wi-Fi request carries the token the device got when it paired. The same JSON works over USB serial, one line at a time marked @tb, with no token. It never returns the calendar address. docs/api.md is the full contract.
      
          
      Pairing
      Approved on 2026-10-04 (details in docs/api.md section 4). So that nobody else on the office Wi-Fi can change your bar, a phone, Mac or script pairs once with a 6-digit code that MiniBar shows only on its own screen: the code lasts 2 minutes and allows 3 tries, only the device that asked can use it, and only someone who can see the bar can read it. One code shows at a time, and Cancel on the device that asked takes it off the bar at once. After two failed pairings in a row (timed out, canceled or out of tries), the bar waits 30 seconds before it shows another, then 1, 2, 4 minutes and so on, up to an hour. A bar keeps up to 10 devices, and a code on its screen holds one of those places until it ends, so the code it shows always works. Plugged in with USB, your Mac pairs on its own, with no code. The Remote lists paired devices with Remove; the Wi-Fi menu's Devices tile forgets them all at once, and with nothing paired it says where to pair (minibar.local). Try it with Pairing in the Simulate panel.
      
          
      Flip
      Turning the bar over always does three things: the layout turns the right way up, any alarm goes quiet, and the Pomodoro starts whatever it's waiting for (the next session or break, or a paused timer). A timer that's already running is left alone, so a flip never skips anything. On a call or meeting screen it also shows the Pomodoro, which sets the call or meeting aside. On a pairing code, or the quick tour, it ends that first, then does the same three things. While the bar is starting up or powering off, the layout still turns with it, and its other jobs wait until it's on.
      
          
      Alarm
      When a session or break ends, the bar flashes and chimes every few seconds until you tap, press a button or flip it, and gives up after a minute. With auto-start on, it chimes once and carries on. During a call or meeting it flashes without the chime. While a pairing code shows, the alarm waits, and it rings once when pairing ends. The mock-up plays the chime in your browser.

      The Mac app · a summary of docs/api.md
      
    
      How the Mac app talks to MiniBar
      
    
      The bar hands out the Mac app itself (the Remote's Download for Mac, Proposed), and its side of the conversation is ready. The app reports one thing, whether you're on a call, and optionally the app's name, with a random install ID so the bar can tell Macs apart. Over Wi-Fi it uses the bar's local web address and a token it got by pairing; when the bar is powered from the Mac it uses the USB cable instead, which needs no token and also works without Wi-Fi.

      • active and client are required. client is a random install ID the app makes once, not a hardware ID or the computer's name; with session and seq it keeps Macs and links apart and drops a late message (sending them is proposed). app is optional: the bar shows up to 24 characters of it.
        • 
      
      • Tokens: every Wi-Fi request carries one. The Mac app's has the call scope (report calls, read the status); the Remote's and scripts' have full. The Remote keeps its token in a cookie the bar sets. Remove one on the Remote's Paired devices, or all of them on the bar.
        • 
      
      • The app sends its state on every change and repeats it every 30 seconds. After 90 seconds without a message, the bar ends On a call on its own, so it can't get stuck.
        • 
      
      • showing is call, meeting, own (your own status, including a call or meeting you set aside) or setup. A dark screen is "screen": "dark", and, Proposed, the quick tour of the controls is "screen": "tour", with everything else still real; a powered-off bar doesn't answer.
        • 
      
      • Every error is {"ok": false, "error": "…", "message": "…", "field": …}: error is a stable code to act on, message is for logs, and field names the field at fault.
        • 
      
      • No reply ever contains the calendar's secret address. Meeting titles are left out when Show meeting titles is off.
        • 
      
      • If both links work, the app prefers USB, with the same client and the next seq.
        • 
      
      • Proposed: the bar's theme is a setting, display.theme: "bold_signal" (the default) or "low_glare_pixel". The Remote reads and changes it with GET and PATCH /api/v1/settings (scope full), as {"display": {"theme": "low_glare_pixel"}}; any other value is 400 bad_value. A change bumps rev in the status, so a switch made on the bar reaches the Remote within about 2 seconds. The Mac app doesn't use it. See docs/api.md 10.3.
        • 
      
      • Proposed: Tap sound is a setting too, sound.tap_sound: true (the default) or false, read as settings.sound.tap_sound and changed with PATCH /api/v1/settings (scope full), as {"sound": {"tap_sound": false}}; anything but true or false is 400 bad_value with "field": "sound.tap_sound". The bar shows "Tap sound on" or "Tap sound off"; a change through the API never makes it click, since only touches on the bar do. See docs/api.md 10.4.
        • 
      
      • Proposed: the help page is a plain page at /help on the office network, outside the API. The bar serves it as it serves the Remote's page, and it needs no pairing, since it holds nothing private. The quick tour's last tip links to it by the bar's IP address, and the Remote's Help link and "How pairing works" open it.
        • 
      
      • Proposed: Download for Mac. The bar carries the Mac app's zip in its own flash and hands it out at GET /mac/app.zip, outside /api/ since it isn't JSON, with no product name in the path; Content-Disposition carries the readable name (MiniBar-1.0.zip). It needs no token, like the Remote's page, and the host check applies. HEAD gets the same headers; one Range gets 206, so a download that broke off resumes; with no app, or a damaged copy, it's a plain-text 404. GET /api/v1/info (and the USB hello) reports the copy as mac_app: null, or state, reason, version, build, bytes, archs, min_macos, file, path and sha256, which is where the Remote's "MiniBar for Mac 1.0 (12) · 1.8 MB" comes from. The Mac app ignores it. See docs/api.md 17.
