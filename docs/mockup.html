Style

Try fonts, colors and screen modes on the screen. Your choices stay in this browser. Copy them when you've found a look you like, and send them to me for the firmware.

The simulator opens with your pick, Bold Signal: Barlow Condensed for headlines and Barlow for small text on solid color fields. Each headline takes the largest of its four sizes that fits (112, 100, 78 or 62 px), and the digits are all one width, so the timer and clock don't jitter. A look you apply or change here is remembered in this browser; one you've never touched follows the default.

Your earlier pick, Low Glare Pixel, is a design direction above, and its Bitcount Prop Single and Handjet come first under Pixel fonts. Pixel fonts snap to whole multiples of their own pixel grid so they stay crisp, and each headline takes the largest of those sizes that fits. Bitcount's digits are drawn in equal-width cells, as the bar's copy of the font draws them, so the timer and clock don't jitter. With a pixel headline font other than Jersey 10 or 15, the line under the headline uses the text font, since the others are too wide or too big for it.

Dark mode is the Low Glare treatment: picking it starts from Low Glare's colors, tints and sentence-case headlines, with the fonts you've chosen. The original look is still here: choose Solid color fields, then Big Shoulders Display and Schibsted Grotesk.

Stage names are the USDA's ripeness classes for fresh tomatoes. Tap one to jump to it.

    How you'd control it
    
        
    
          
    Touch
    Tap cycles status, a swipe steps back or forward, and holding opens a quick menu with light, calendar, Wi-Fi and power. On the Pomodoro screen, a tap starts or pauses the timer and holding opens timer options. On a call or meeting screen, a tap goes back to your own status; the call or meeting is set aside until it ends. A dark screen wakes on the first tap, and every action shows a short confirmation. On a pairing code, a tap, swipe or hold only cancels it.
    
          
    Pomodoro
    25-minute focus sessions, 5-minute breaks and a 15-minute break after every fourth session, all adjustable. Focus shows in orange so people know not to interrupt; breaks turn the bar teal or blue to say it's a good moment. Each finished session earns a red tomato, upcoming sessions show as faded tomatoes, and the one in progress ripens like a real tomato as you focus: color breaks at the bottom and spreads up to the stem, through yellow and orange to red. The timer keeps running when you switch to another status, with a small countdown in the corner, and the bar jumps back to it when a phase ends. A call or calendar meeting pauses it, and the bar makes no sound while one is on. A ringing alarm goes quiet and chimes once when it's over. Ticking during focus is optional and off by default: one gentle tick a second, Soft or Medium, set on the Remote or under Settings in the timer menu. It's silent while the timer is paused, on breaks, while the alarm rings, during calls and meetings, while the screen is dark, and when the bar is powered off.
    
          
    Side buttons
    BOOT jumps to the next status without looking; on a call or meeting screen it goes back to your own status, like a tap. A press of PWR turns the screen off and on, holding it for 3 seconds powers the bar off, and a press turns it back on. If the screen is dark or an alarm is ringing, the first press of either button only wakes the screen or silences the alarm. On a pairing code, BOOT cancels it, and a PWR press cancels it and turns the screen off.
    
          
    Power
    On battery, the software is what keeps the bar switched on, so it also handles switching off: hold PWR for 3 seconds, or use Power in the quick menu, which also has Restart. A watchdog restarts the bar if the software ever freezes, so it can't get stuck on, and it shuts itself down before the battery runs flat. On USB the bar always has power, so powering off puts it into a deep sleep until you press PWR. While it's off, the Remote page can't reach it and says so.
    
          
    Wi-Fi setup
    On first start the bar shows a QR code. Scanning it joins your phone to the bar's own network, where a page lets you choose the office Wi-Fi, enter the password and paste your calendar's secret iCal address (optional). You can also skip Wi-Fi; statuses and the Pomodoro work without it, but the calendar and the Remote don't. Once it's on the Wi-Fi, the bar shows its name and address (TinyBar 2A1C at tinybar.local) on the Connected screen and in the Wi-Fi menu. A second TinyBar on the same network takes the next free address (tinybar-2.local) and shows that instead, so you can tell them apart. The default name, TinyBar plus the last four characters of the bar's ID, is proposed.
    
          
    Phone remote
    The device serves a small page at tinybar.local on your Wi-Fi, like the Remote panel on this page, with the bar's name at the top. The first time, it asks you to pair: TinyBar shows a code, and you type it on the phone. Its Automatic status section has a switch for each source, holds your calendar's secret address, and shows what the bar is showing and why. Paired devices lists every phone, Mac and script that can control the bar, each with Remove. A message with characters the bar can't draw, such as an emoji, says which ones before you send it. Characters nobody can see (an odd-width space, a zero-width space, a direction mark) are tidied away rather than refused, and accents typed as a separate mark are joined to their letter.
    
          
    Calendar
    Paste your calendar's secret iCal address on the Remote, or during Wi-Fi setup (Google Calendar › Settings › your calendar › Integrate calendar › "Secret address in iCal format"). Meetings then show as In a meeting on their own and fill in Next up. Once saved, the address is never shown again; the Remote shows a short masked form with Replace and Remove. Show meeting titles is off by default, so the bar shows times only, and events marked Private never show a title. The bar syncs about every 10 minutes, or when you choose Sync now, and switches at each meeting's exact start and end time. Skipping Wi-Fi turns the calendar off; if Wi-Fi drops, the bar follows its last synced copy.
    
          
    Automatic status
    Two sources, each with its own switch on the Remote: meetings from your calendar, and calls from your Mac. On a call wins over In a meeting, which wins over your own status. A tap, swipe, BOOT, flip or a choice on the Remote sets the call or meeting aside until it ends, and a small headset or calendar glyph stays in the corner. Show again, in the quick menu, the timer menu or on the Remote, brings it back, and the next call or meeting always takes over again. A call or meeting that takes over pauses the Pomodoro, and the bar makes no sound while one is on.
    
          
    Mac app
    A small menu-bar app sets On a call whenever your Mac's mic or camera is in use, over USB when the bar is powered from the Mac and otherwise over Wi-Fi. Only whether you're on a call, and optionally the app's name, leaves the Mac. To use Wi-Fi it pairs once: plugged into the Mac with USB it pairs on its own, or in its menu you choose Connect…, then Pair Over Wi-Fi, and type the code the bar shows. How the Mac app talks to TinyBar
    
          
    Local API
    The bar answers on your Wi-Fi at tinybar.local, under /api/v1/: POST /api/v1/call sets or ends On a call, and GET /api/v1/status says what the bar is showing and which sources are on. With pairing, every Wi-Fi request carries the token the device got when it paired. The same JSON works over USB serial, one line at a time marked @tb, with no token. It never returns the calendar address. docs/api.md is the full contract.
    
          
    Pairing
    Approved on 2026-10-04 (details in docs/api.md section 4). So that nobody else on the office Wi-Fi can change your bar, a phone, Mac or script pairs once with a 6-digit code that TinyBar shows only on its own screen: the code lasts 2 minutes and allows 3 tries, only the device that asked can use it, and only someone who can see the bar can read it. One code shows at a time, and Cancel on the device that asked takes it off the bar at once. After two failed pairings in a row (timed out, canceled or out of tries), the bar waits 30 seconds before it shows another, then 1, 2, 4 minutes and so on, up to an hour. A bar keeps up to 10 devices, and a code on its screen holds one of those places until it ends, so the code it shows always works. Plugged in with USB, your Mac pairs on its own, with no code. The Remote lists paired devices with Remove; the Wi-Fi menu's Devices tile forgets them all at once, and with nothing paired it says where to pair (tinybar.local). Try it with Pairing in the Simulate panel.
    
          
    Flip
    Turning the bar over always does three things: the layout turns the right way up, any alarm goes quiet, and the Pomodoro starts whatever it's waiting for (the next session or break, or a paused timer). A timer that's already running is left alone, so a flip never skips anything. On a call or meeting screen it also shows the Pomodoro, which sets the call or meeting aside. On a pairing code, it cancels pairing first, then does the same three things.
    
          
    Alarm
    When a session or break ends, the bar flashes and chimes every few seconds until you tap, press a button or flip it, and gives up after a minute. With auto-start on, it chimes once and carries on. During a call or meeting it flashes without the chime. While a pairing code shows, the alarm waits, and it rings once when pairing ends. The mock-up plays the chime in your browser.
    
        
    
      
    
      
    
        
    Hardware it uses
    
        
    
          
    Display
    3.49" IPS, 172 × 640, AXS15231B over QSPI
    
          
    Touch
    AXS15231B over I²C
    
          
    MCU
    ESP32-S3R8, 8 MB PSRAM, 16 MB flash
    
          
    Clock
    PCF85063 RTC, synced over NTP
    
          
    Motion
    QMI8658 6-axis IMU
    
          
    Audio
    ES8311 codec + speaker
    
          
    Power
    USB-C, or 3.7 V Li-ion on MX1.25 (pin 2 +), charges at 1.2 A

    How the Mac app talks to TinyBar
    
    
    The bar's side is ready now, so the Mac app can be added without changing the bar. The app reports one thing, whether you're on a call, and optionally the app's name, with a random install ID so the bar can tell Macs apart. Over Wi-Fi it uses the bar's local web address and a token it got by pairing; when the bar is powered from the Mac it uses the USB cable instead, which needs no token and also works without Wi-Fi.

    • active and client are required. client is a random install ID the app makes once, not a hardware ID or the computer's name; with session and seq it keeps Macs and links apart and drops a late message (sending them is proposed). app is optional: the bar shows up to 24 characters of it.
      • 
      
    • Tokens: every Wi-Fi request carries one. The Mac app's has the call scope (report calls, read the status); the Remote's and scripts' have full. The Remote keeps its token in a cookie the bar sets. Remove one on the Remote's Paired devices, or all of them on the bar.
      • 
      
    • The app sends its state on every change and repeats it every 30 seconds. After 90 seconds without a message, the bar ends On a call on its own, so it can't get stuck.
      • 
      
    • showing is call, meeting, own (your own status, including a call or meeting you set aside) or setup. A dark screen is "screen": "dark"; a powered-off bar doesn't answer.
      • 
      
    • Every error is {"ok": false, "error": "…", "message": "…", "field": …}: error is a stable code to act on, message is for logs, and field names the field at fault.
      • 
      
    • No reply ever contains the calendar's secret address. Meeting titles are left out when Show meeting titles is off.
      • 
      
    • If both links work, the app prefers USB, with the same client and the next seq.
      • 
    
    
    
    docs/api.md is the full contract between the bar and everything that talks to it (every endpoint and error code, the USB details, pairing's limits), and it wins wherever this summary differs.
