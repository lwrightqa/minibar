MiniBar · controls guide

Tap, swipe, hold, flip.

MiniBar tells the people around your desk whether it’s a good time to talk. You run it with one finger on its screen, two side buttons, and by turning it over. This page shows what each of those does, and how the screens connect. Every picture is a real screen from the mock-up, at the bar’s 640 × 172 shape.

The controls

Seven things you can do. Each has one main job everywhere, plus a few screens where it does something more specific.

On the screen, and the whole bar

Tap

Touch and lift within 550 ms

Next status.

  • On the Pomodoro, it starts or pauses the timer.
  • On a call or meeting, it goes back to your own status.
  • On a menu, it runs the tile you tap. A tap off the tiles closes the menu.

Swipe

Slide at least 40 px

Swipe left for the next status, right for the previous one.

  • Left and right are read as the layout faces you, so they still mean the same after a flip.
  • On a call or meeting, it counts from your own status and sets the call aside.
  • On a menu a swipe is ignored, so it can’t run the tile it ends on.

Hold

Keep still for 550 ms

Opens the menu for the screen you’re on.

  • On a status, call or meeting screen: the quick menu.
  • On the Pomodoro: the timer menu. On Wi-Fi setup: Setup options.
  • Inside a sub-menu, it goes back to the quick menu. It acts at 550 ms, before you lift.

Flip

Turn the bar over

Turns the layout the right way up, silences any alarm, and starts whatever the Pomodoro is waiting for.

  • A running timer is left alone (“Focus already running”).
  • On a call or meeting, it also shows the Pomodoro and sets the call aside.
  • On a pairing code, it cancels pairing first.

Side buttons

BOOT

Press the upper side button

Next status, without looking.

  • On a call or meeting, it goes back to your own status, like a tap.
  • On a ringing alarm, it only silences it (“Alarm off”).
  • On a menu, it closes the menu and goes to the next status.

PWR press

Press the lower button, let go within 0.4 s

Turns the screen dark, or back on.

  • Timers, calls and meetings carry on while it’s dark; their toasts wait.
  • On a ringing alarm, the first press only silences it.
  • On a pairing code, it also cancels pairing.

PWR hold

Keep PWR down for 3 s

Powers the bar off. A PWR press starts it again.

  • KEEP HOLDING appears at 0.4 s. Let go before 3 s and nothing changes.
  • On USB, off is a deep sleep woken by PWR.
  • The Pomodoro run stops; today’s tomatoes and paired devices stay.

Rules everywhere

These hold on every screen, so you can guess what will happen even on one you haven’t seen.

Every action confirms itself.
A toast shows at the bottom for 1.6 s (“Paused”, “Call set aside · hold to show it again”), or the change is the confirmation: a new status, the Light tile’s new level, a dark screen. Anything that happens while the screen is dark, or a menu, setup or a pairing code is up, shows once it’s free.
A flip always does three things.
It turns the layout the right way up, silences any alarm, and starts whatever the Pomodoro is waiting for: a ready set, the next phase, or a paused timer. A running timer is left alone. Only a powered-off bar or the start-up splash ignores a flip.
Nothing is a dead end.
Every screen has a way back. Every menu has a light Done or Back tile and closes after 8 s untouched. On a dark screen, the first tap, swipe, hold or BOOT only wakes it.
The alarm moves on with a tap or a flip.
When a session or break ends, the screen flashes and chimes every 4 s for up to a minute. A tap or flip starts the next phase. BOOT or PWR only silence it. A hold silences it and opens the timer menu; a swipe silences it and changes status.
Calls and meetings wait their turn.
They take over your status, but never a menu, Wi-Fi setup, the power screens or a pairing code; they show once the screen is free. While one is on, the bar makes no sound and a running Pomodoro pauses.
Higher on the list wins.
Top first: the start-up splash, Wi-Fi setup, a pairing code, On a call, In a meeting from the calendar, then your own status. A choice you make during a call or meeting wins until it ends.

How screens connect

Each diagram uses the bar’s real screens. Arrows are labeled with what moves you along: a control, or something that happens on its own, like a timer ending or a call starting.

The status cycle

Seven statuses in a loop. You step through them with a tap, a swipe or BOOT; the Remote’s status buttons jump straight to any of them.

The Pomodoro

25 minutes of focus, a 5 minute break, and after every fourth focus a 15 minute break. Each finished focus earns a red tomato; the one in progress ripens from the bottom up.

Calls and meetings

Two sources can set your status for you: a calendar meeting, and a call your Mac notices. You can always override them.

Menus

Hold the screen to open the menu for where you are. Only taps act on a menu.

Screen and power

A short PWR press is the screen switch. Holding PWR is the power switch.

Wi-Fi setup

On first start the bar shows a QR code that joins your phone to its MiniBar-Setup network, where a page lets you pick the office Wi-Fi.

Pairing

Before a phone, a Mac or a script can change the bar over Wi-Fi, it pairs once with a code shown only on the bar, so only someone who can see the bar can pair.

Menus

A row of tiles over the dark surface. Bordered tiles act when tapped; borderless ones are read-only; the light tile closes or goes back.

Quick menu

Hold on any status screen, On a call, In a meeting (calendar), Connected, or any sub-menu.

  • LIGHT · 70% · tap to changeSteps the backlight 40%, 70%, 100%; the menu stays open and the tile shows the new level (no toast).
  • CALENDAR · Sync · synced 2m agoSyncs now (“Calendar synced”) and closes. Other states: “Sync…” “syncing now” (“Still syncing”); “Off” “needs Wi-Fi” (“No Wi-Fi, can’t sync”); “Off” “add it on the Remote” (“Add your calendar on the Remote”).
  • SET ASIDE · Show again · the call (replaces Calendar while a call or meeting is set aside)Brings the call or meeting back (“Showing the call again”).
  • WI-FI · On · Office-WiFi (offline: Off · tap to set up)Opens the Wi-Fi menu.
  • POWER · USB · off or restartOpens the Power menu.
  • CLOSE · Done · or wait 8 s (light tile)Closes the menu.

Closes: Done, a tap off the tiles, 8 s untouched, BOOT (also next status, or back to your status on a call or meeting), PWR (dark), a flip, or a pairing code arriving.

Timer menu

  • STOP · End · keeps tomatoesEnds the run, keeps today’s count, returns to the status you had before (“Pomodoro stopped”).
  • SETTINGS · More · auto-start, tickingOpens Timer settings.
  • SET ASIDE · Show again · the call (replaces Settings while a call or meeting is set aside)Brings the call or meeting back (“Showing the call again”).
  • CLOSE · Done · or wait 8 s (light tile)Closes the menu.

Closes: Done, a tap off the tiles, 8 s, BOOT (also next status), PWR (dark), a flip, or a pairing code.

Open issues

Where the mock-up still differs from the rules above. The guide describes the intended behavior and marks these spots.

  • IssueThe mock-up’s Pomodoro diagram. They say the alarm screens advance on “tap, flip or button”, but BOOT and PWR only silence the alarm and leave the waiting screen; a second BOOT goes to the next status. The controls table is right. Fix: label the arrows “tap or flip” and say “either button silences it”.
  • IssueKEEP HOLDING and POWERING OFF. Taps and swipes pass through KEEP HOLDING and POWERING OFF to the screen underneath, changing the status unseen (verified: a tap during POWERING OFF moved Busy to In a meeting), and BOOT does too. Fix: while the hold overlay shows, ignore touches and BOOT (return early in the pointerdown, pointerup and BOOT handlers when #holdOv isn’t hidden); the firmware should drop the same input.
  • IssueBack tiles in the Wi-Fi and Power menus. The Wi-Fi and Power menus’ light tile reads “Back” but closes the whole menu, while Settings’ Back and Forget devices’ Keep (“back to Wi-Fi”) return to their parent. Fix: make those Back tiles return to the quick menu, with the foot “to the quick menu”.
  • IssueBOOT on Setup options. BOOT leaves the menu open and shows the setup screen’s toast over it (“Scan the code with your phone”). Fix: BOOT closes the menu first, as on every other menu.
  • NoteA flip during Wi-Fi setup. The flip rule runs under the QR code and shows a Pomodoro toast (“Focus already running”) about a screen you can’t see. Suggest no Pomodoro toast on setup screens; the layout turn is its own confirmation.
  • NoteThe mock-up’s Wi-Fi setup caption. The Wi-Fi setup caption says holding on Connected opens the quick menu; with the Pomodoro as your status it opens the timer menu. Suggest “opens the menu for your status”.
