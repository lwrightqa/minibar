TinyBar · Low Glare in pixel fonts

Low Glare, set in pixel fonts

The Low Glare layout and colors in seven pixel-font setups. You picked option G, Bitcount with Handjet, which comes first. Only the fonts and their sizes change. Each option shows the same three screens: Busy, a Pomodoro focus session and On a call. The current Low Glare follows, for comparison.

Every pixel-font size is a whole-number multiple of the font’s pixel grid (the “4x” next to a size), so letters stay crisp on the device. Screens are 640 × 172, shown 1:1 in any window wider than about 720 px; narrower windows scale them down, which softens the pixels. The real screen is only 8.6 cm wide, so small text there looks smaller than it does here. Ligatures are off and digits are tabular, as they will be on the device. “Soft pixels” is the share of partly lit pixels once the font is converted for the device; 0% means perfectly sharp.

Your pick · October 4

Bitcount Prop Single, with Handjet for small text

You picked option D’s round-dot headlines with Handjet for the small text. The team set that up as option G, first below, and fixed what the font study had flagged:

  • The countdown holds still. The bar gets its own copy of Bitcount in which every digit is the same width, so the timer and clock don’t jitter.
  • Small text is pixel-sharp. Handjet is only sharp at 34 px and up, and 34 px doesn’t fit the side column, so the bar uses a copy of Handjet with every element on a whole pixel at 17 px.
  • Real copy fits. The line under the headline moves to Handjet, since option D’s square-dot line runs out of room with “break at 12:55 PM”. The side value keeps D’s square dots, one size down, so “1h 5m” fits.
What option G means for the build
  • Fonts: TinyBarBitcount-Round.ttf and TinyBarBitcount-Square.ttf, built with tools/fonts/equalize_digits.py (--grid 100 --colon --tnum-shapes, as in its README), and Handjet-Snap17.ttf, Handjet’s solid squares with every element on a whole pixel. Convert with --autohint-off.
  • Round dots at 2 bits per pixel: 100, 80, 60, 50 and 40 px with the full character set, and 120 px with digits and colon only. Square dots at 40 px and Handjet at 17 and 34 px at 1 bit. About 105 KB in all.
  • Headlines use 100 px, or the first of 80, 60 or 50 px that fits the 408 px column. Meeting titles drop to 40 px before they’re cut off with an ellipsis.
  • Kicker and labels in capitals with 1 px letter-spacing. Menu tiles need 10 px inner padding instead of 13 px, so “Medium” and “QR code” fit at 34 px.

The team’s earlier recommendation

Before you chose, the UX designer, lead developer and product manager each ranked the options, and all three picked option A, Jersey 15 with Micro 5: one big, solid word that reads best from across the office, real lowercase for sentence case, and perfectly sharp pixels on the device. Runners-up were F, Jersey 15 with Atkinson, and C, Handjet.

Option G

Bitcount + Handjet

Round dots, slim text. Option D’s round LED dots for the status word, the timer and the clock, and Handjet, a slim pixel sans, for every line you read. The dots carry the status across the room. Handjet keeps the small print crisp and calm.

Bitcount in two settings, as in option D. The bar gets its own copy of each with equal-width digits, the 1 with a base as option D showed it and even space around the colon, so the timer and clock hold still. Handjet is a copy of its solid squares with every element moved onto a whole pixel, the only way it’s sharp at 17 px (embedded in this page). Small capitals have 1 px letter-spacing.

Lowercase Weak headline, very good text. The headline’s lowercase is option D’s: an x-height of 5 on a 6-dot cap and 1-dot descenders. Everything below it is Handjet, with ascenders taller than its capitals and 3-pixel descenders. The y in Busy clears the sub line by 23 px.

Pros

  • The round-dot headline you liked, with Low Glare’s calm layout around it. Busy is 220 px wide, the timer 348 px and On a call 390 of 408 px.
  • The timer and clock hold still. In the bar’s copy of Bitcount every digit is 84 px wide at 120 px, with the 1 that has a base, as option D showed it, and a dot of space each side of the colon (checked in the converted font).
  • Everything you read is one crisp font. The snapped Handjet converts with 0% soft pixels at 17 and 34 px, and both sizes together take 4 KB.
  • Room for real copy: “break at 12:55 PM” uses 235 of 408 px, and the longest side-column foot, “turned off on the Remote”, 145 of 154 px.
  • Handjet’s narrow letters and real descenders suit the side column and sentence case.

Cons

  • Bitcount is wide, so only short words fit at 100 px. Available steps down to 80 px, In a meeting to 60 px, Back at 12:30 to 50 px, and meeting titles to 60 or 40 px. The headline changes size more from screen to screen than in any other option.
  • Small text is 17 px with 11 px capitals, about 1.5 mm on the bar: for reading up close. Handjet’s next sharp size, 34 px, doesn’t fit the side column.
  • The line under the headline is Handjet, not option D’s square dots, so it has less presence from across the room. D’s line would need shorter copy and would cut off longer meeting details.
  • Round dots are always soft-edged on the device (23–27% of lit pixels at 60–120 px, more at 40 and 50 px), and they take the most flash of the seven options: about 105 KB at 2 bits per pixel. That’s still under 1% of the bar’s flash.
  • The firmware converts three prepared font files instead of the downloads: Bitcount’s two settings with equal-width digits, and the snapped Handjet. All three are built.
  • Handjet’s I and l are identical, and Bitcount’s B and 8 can briefly read as 6, as in option D.

Option A

Jersey 15

Scoreboard. Chunky, condensed letters with 2-pixel strokes, like a stadium scoreboard or a sports jersey. Bold and friendly.

TextFontSize

TimerJersey 15135 px 5x

Small textMicro 522 px 2x

Small text has 2 px letter-spacing on the capitals. Micro 5 is by the same designer as Jersey 15.

Lowercase Good. An x-height of 11 on a 15-pixel cap and real 4-pixel descenders, with a clear a, e, g and s. The y in Busy clears the sub line by 21 px.

Pros

  • Closest in size to the original: Busy is 184 px wide, the timer 235 px and On a call 320 of 408 px.
  • The sub line is the headline font itself at 1x, so the whole main column is one face.
  • Pixel-exact on the device: 0% soft pixels at every Jersey size.
  • The strongest color from across the room of the pixel options.
  • The small text matches, since Micro 5 comes from the same designer.

Cons

  • At 108 px, “In a meeting” (484 px) and “Back at 12:30” (508 px) don’t fit the 408 px column, so those screens use the next sharp size, 81 px.
  • Its default digits are proportional (the 1 is narrower), so the countdown would jitter. The firmware has to swap in the font’s tabular digits before converting. The screens here already show them.
  • The Pomodoro sub line has only 9 px to spare. A longer one would need the small font or get cut.
  • I and l are the same bar, and O and 0 are nearly the same.
  • The heaviest look of the six, and less quiet than Barlow Condensed.
Option B

VT323

Terminal. The DEC VT320 terminal face: tall, thin one-pixel strokes on a wide monospace grid. Quiet and a bit nerdy.

VT323’s pixel is 12.5 px at 1x, so its only sharp sizes are multiples of 25. Small text has 2 px letter-spacing on the capitals.

Lowercase Excellent. An x-height of 5 on a 7-row cap and 2-row descenders, with a real g, p and y. The y in Busy comes within 9 px of the sub line, the closest of the six, but doesn’t touch.

Pros

  • The thinnest strokes and the least lit area: the calmest pixel look.
  • Excellent lowercase.
  • Monospace digits, so the timer never jitters. I, l and 1 all look different.
  • Tiny5, used below the headline, is pixel-exact on the device.

Cons

  • Not a true square-pixel font. It imitates a CRT, so on the device 13% of headline pixels are soft at 125 px, 16.5% at 100 px and 29% at 50 px. It looks soft-edged rather than blocky.
  • On a call drops to 100 px, because at 125 px it would be 437 px wide.
  • VT323 is too wide for its own sub line and small text, so those are Tiny5. Its wide, chunky letters contrast with VT323’s narrow, thin ones.
  • The Tiny5 sub line fills all 408 px, with no margin.
  • Thin strokes carry less color from a distance.
Option C

Handjet

Slim sign. A tall, condensed pixel sans with one-element strokes, like a modern LED sign or a ticket machine. The closest pixel cousin of Barlow Condensed, and the calmest of the six.

TextFontSize
All headlinesHandjet102 px 3x
Side valueHandjet34 px 1x
Sub lineMicro 522 px 2x
Small textMicro 522 px 2x

Handjet’s default solid squares (element shape 2, grid 1). It’s a variable font, so the firmware converts a static copy, which is ready. Small text has 2 px letter-spacing on the capitals.

Lowercase Very good. An x-height of 8 on an 11-element cap, ascenders of 12 and descenders of 3. The y in Busy clears the sub line by 18 px.

Pros

  • Condensed: On a call uses only 261 of 408 px, and one size (102 px) fits all three headlines. In a meeting just fits at that size too, with nothing to spare (408 of 408 px).
  • Ascenders taller than the capitals give strong word shapes in sentence case.
  • Tabular digits by default, so no timer jitter.
  • Thin strokes keep glare low.
  • Pixel-exact on the device: 0% soft pixels at 34, 68 and 102 px.
  • A free variant: the same font makes a round-dot LED sign (below), though its dots are soft on the device (36%).

Cons

  • Sharp only at 34, 68 and 102 px. 17 px is soft, so the sub line and small text use Micro 5, which makes the sub line small (10 px capitals, 282 px wide).
  • Back at 12:30 on the Away screen is 438 px wide at 102 px, 30 px too wide for the main column. It needs the next sharp size down, 68 px, where it’s 292 px wide.
  • The side value at 34 px has 22 px capitals, smaller than the original’s 29 px. At 68 px it would run into the line below.
  • Its thin strokes carry less color across the room than Jersey 15.
  • I and l are identical.

Jersey 15 + Atkinson

Pixel headline, clean text. Jersey 15 for the headline and side value, and Low Glare’s own Atkinson Hyperlegible Next for everything else. A direct test of whether pixel small text is worth it.

Pros

  • Atkinson needs anti-aliasing on the device and takes more flash memory than a 2x pixel font.
  • The Jersey timer still needs the tabular-digit swap.

Notes

For the firmware

  1. Convert at whole-number sizes only: every size on this page is a whole-number scale of its font.
  2. Convert pixel fonts with --autohint-off. The autohinter moved Bitcount’s crossbars by half a pixel and changed nothing in the others.
  3. Handjet and Bitcount are variable fonts and need static copies (ready in the team folder). Option G’s copies are also adjusted: Bitcount with equal-width digits, and Handjet with every element on a whole pixel at 17 px.
  4. Jersey 15 and Micro 5 have proportional digits by default. Swap in their tabular digits before converting so the timer doesn’t jitter. Bitcount’s tabular digits don’t fix it: its 1, 6 and 7 are still one dot narrower than the other digits. VT323, Tiny5, Handjet and Press Start 2P are already tabular. Option G’s copies of Bitcount fix it with tools/fonts/equalize_digits.py: every digit gets the same width and is centered, the colon gets matching space, and kerning between digits is removed.
  5. Round dots look the same at 2 bits per pixel as at 4 and take about 40% less flash. Fonts with 0% soft pixels can be stored at 1 bit.
  6. Letter-spacing must be whole font pixels (2 px at 2x).

Also tried, and why not

  • Silkscreen: no real lowercase, so no sentence case.
  • Pixelify Sans, DotGothic16: off their own pixel grid, so blurry on the device.
  • Geist Pixel: a very fine grid that lands on whole pixels only near 79 px.
  • Coral Pixels: a color font that ignores the text color.
  • Rubik Pixels: a texture of tiny dots.
  • Jacquard 12 and 24, Jacquarda Bastarda 9: blackletter.
  • Sixtyfour, Workbench: rounded CRT pixels with gaps, and as wide as Press Start 2P.
  • Doto: its colon looks like a double dagger, and it can’t set the sub line.
  • Bitcount Prop Double: its 8 reads as a 6.
  • Jersey 10: sharp only at 56 and 112 px. Jersey 20 and 25: too wide for the sub line.
  • Bytesized: tiny 3 × 4 letters with no descenders.
  • Tiny5 and Micro 5 as headlines: crude at large sizes. Both work well as small text.
  • Handjet’s round dots as option G’s small text: at 17 and 34 px a dot is 1 or 2 px, too small to be round, so the device draws a plain or a gray stroke. Its dots only show from 68 px.
